# Under the Scope with Pastor Billy Daws
## Episode — "When You Question God: Habakkuk Under the Scope"

**Runtime target:** 22–26 min
**One-line premise:** A prophet brings his hardest questions about God out into
the open, and God is not offended by the questions — He answers them, and teaches
Habakkuk (and us) how to trust Him when nothing makes sense.
**Primary text:** Habakkuk (whole book) — NKJV. Anchor verse: Habakkuk 2:4.
**Bridge verses:** Hebrews 11:6; Romans 8:1.

---

## COLD OPEN (0:00–0:30)

Have you ever been afraid to say to God what you were really thinking?

Not the polite prayer. The real one. The one that says, *"Lord, I don't
understand what You're doing. How long is this going to go on? And why does it
look like the people doing wrong are winning?"*

There's a whole book of the Bible written by a man who prayed that prayer out
loud. And God didn't strike him down. God answered him. Today we're putting
Habakkuk under the scope.

## INTRO (0:30–2:15)

Welcome back to *Under the Scope*. I'm Billy Daws, and on this show we take the
things of God, the things of Scripture, the things of everyday life — and we
look at them up close. Not from a distance. Not the way we assume they are.
Under the scope, where you can see what's really there.

And if you're somebody who loves God but you're tired — if you're doing the
right things and it feels like it isn't working, if you've got questions you've
been afraid to ask — this episode is for you.

Because Habakkuk is a short book. Three chapters. Most people skip right past it.
But it's one of the most honest conversations with God in the whole Bible. It's
a man arguing with God — and coming out the other side worshiping. Let's see how
he got there.

---

## SEGMENT 1 — The honest question (2:15–8:00)

**Key point:** God can handle your questions. Faith is not the absence of them.

Set the scene. Habakkuk is a prophet in Judah, roughly six hundred years before
Christ. And everywhere he looks, his own nation is falling apart. Violence.
Injustice. The law is powerless. The wicked are running the show. And he's been
praying about it — and it seems like heaven is silent.

So listen to how he opens. Habakkuk 1:2, NKJV:

> "O LORD, how long shall I cry, And You will not hear? Even cry out to You,
> 'Violence!' And You will not save."

Now put that under the scope for a second. That's a prophet — a man of God —
telling God, *"How long? Are You even listening?"* And it's right there in
Scripture. God preserved it. Which tells me something: God is not nervous about
your honest questions. He'd rather have your honest cry than your polite
distance.

And here's the turn. God answers — but the answer is harder than the silence.
God says, in verse 5:

> "Look among the nations and watch— Be utterly astounded! For I will work a
> work in your days Which you would not believe, though it were told you."

And what's the work? God says He's raising up the Babylonians — the Chaldeans —
a ruthless, godless empire, to come and judge Judah. In other words: *"You asked
Me to do something about the evil? I'm going to use someone even more evil to do
it."*

And that just breaks Habakkuk. Because now the question is bigger. Now it's not
just "why the silence" — it's "God, how can You, who are holy, use the wicked to
punish people more righteous than they are?" Look at 1:13:

> "You are of purer eyes than to behold evil, And cannot look on wickedness.
> Why do You look on those who deal treacherously, And hold Your tongue when the
> wicked devours A person more righteous than he?"

Have you ever been there? You brought God a problem, and His answer raised a
harder one? That's Habakkuk. That's a lot of us. Hold that thought — because
what he does next is the whole point of this show.

---

## SEGMENT 2 — The watchtower (the scope) (8:00–15:00)

**Key point:** When you don't understand, don't walk away — take up your post
and wait for God to speak.

Habakkuk 2:1. This is my favorite verse in the book, and honestly it's the verse
that this whole show is built on. Listen:

> "I will stand my watch And set myself on the rampart, And watch to see what He
> will say to me, And what I will answer when I am corrected."

Do you see what he's doing? He's climbing the watchtower. He's taking his post.
He's saying, *"I've asked my question. Now I'm going to get up where I can see,
and I'm going to watch — I'm going to keep my eye to the scope — until God
answers."*

That's the difference between doubt that destroys you and doubt that draws you
closer. One walks away. The other climbs the tower and waits. Habakkuk doesn't
stop believing God is there. He just says, "I'm going to keep watching until I
see what He's doing."

And God answers. Verse 2:

> "Write the vision And make it plain on tablets, That he may run who reads it."

And then verse 3 — this is for everybody in a season of waiting:

> "For the vision is yet for an appointed time... Though it tarries, wait for it;
> Because it will surely come, It will not tarry."

God's timing is not slow. It's appointed. It will not be one minute late.

And then comes the heartbeat of the whole book. Verse 4. Underline it:

> "Behold the proud, His soul is not upright in him; But the just shall live by
> his faith."

**"The just shall live by his faith."** Church, that one line is so important
that the Holy Spirit quotes it three times in the New Testament — in Romans, in
Galatians, and in Hebrews. It's the sentence the Reformation was built on. It
means this: the proud man trusts himself, and he'll collapse. But the righteous
man — he lives. How? By faith. By trusting God when he can't yet see the end.

And that connects straight to Hebrews 11:6:

> "But without faith it is impossible to please Him, for he who comes to God must
> believe that He is, and that He is a rewarder of those who diligently seek
> Him."

Faith isn't a feeling that everything's fine. Faith is staying on the
watchtower. Faith is believing God is who He says He is — and that He rewards
the ones who keep seeking Him — even in the dark. That's what Habakkuk is
learning up on that rampart.

---

## SEGMENT 3 — "Yet I will rejoice" (15:00–21:00)

**Key point:** Real faith can worship before the circumstances change.

Now watch where Habakkuk ends up. Chapter 3 is his prayer. And by the end,
nothing about his situation has improved. The Babylonians are still coming.
Judgment is still on the way. The economy of his whole world is about to
collapse. And listen to what he says — 3:17 through 19, and I want you to hear
the whole thing:

> "Though the fig tree may not blossom, Nor fruit be on the vines; Though the
> labor of the olive may fail, And the fields yield no food; Though the flock may
> be cut off from the fold, And there be no herd in the stalls— Yet I will
> rejoice in the LORD, I will joy in the God of my salvation. The LORD God is my
> strength; He will make my feet like deer's feet, And He will make me walk on
> my high hills."

Put "yet" under the scope. *Though* the fig tree doesn't blossom — *yet* I will
rejoice. He doesn't say "when things get better, I'll praise God." He says even
if the fields are empty, even if the stalls are bare, even if I lose everything
— *yet.* Yet I will rejoice in the LORD.

That's a man who started the book saying "how long, O Lord?" and ended it saying
"the LORD God is my strength." He didn't get there by getting answers to every
question. He got there by taking his questions up the watchtower and meeting God
there.

And notice — he rejoices in "the God of my salvation." He's not resting in the
harvest. He's resting in the Savior. For us, on this side of the cross, that
Savior has a name: Jesus. And that's why Romans 8:1 can say:

> "There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus, who
> do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit."

The fig tree may fail. The account may run dry. But if you are in Christ, the
one thing that could truly ruin you — condemnation before a holy God — has been
taken off the table. That's a rock you can rejoice on when everything else is
shaking.

---

## APPLICATION / TAKEAWAY (21:00–23:30)

So here's what I want you to walk out of here with. Three things, quick.

One — **bring God the honest question.** Don't hide it. He already knows it.
Habakkuk said "how long," out loud, and God met him there.

Two — **then get on the watchtower.** Don't let the question walk you away from
God. Let it drive you up higher, where you can watch to see what He'll say. Open
the Word. Pray it out. Wait for it. It will not tarry.

Three — **decide your "yet" before the storm hits.** "Though the fig tree may not
blossom — yet I will rejoice." Settle it now, so that when the hard season comes,
your faith already knows where it stands. The just shall live by his faith.
