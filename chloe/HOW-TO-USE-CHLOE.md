# How to Use Chloe (plain-English guide)

Chloe is your research assistant — like having a seminary study partner on call.
You **talk to her like a person**, in plain English, and she gathers, organizes,
and hands back everything you'd want before you study a passage.

---

## Reaching Chloe

Come back to this project (the `lift-faith-website` workspace in Claude Code).
Then either:

- **Type `/chloe`**, or
- **Just say her name** — e.g. *"Chloe, prepare everything on Romans 8."*

Once she's in, you just keep talking.

---

## The one thing to remember

Say **"Prepare everything on ___"** and Chloe hands you the full packet — all
eleven pieces at once:

> **You:** Chloe, prepare everything on Romans 8.
> **Chloe:** *(a few minutes later)* Here's the full brief — historical
> background, outline, word studies, cross-references, maps, archaeology,
> illustrations, quotes, application ideas, the difficult passages with the main
> views, and discussion questions. Want me to save it?

Or ask for just one piece when that's all you need.

---

## The rhythm: ask → gather → react → go deeper

You don't have to get it all in one sentence. Go back and forth:

> **You:** Chloe, historical background and outline for Philippians.
> **Chloe:** *(delivers both)* Want the word studies next?
> **You:** Yes — and go deeper on *joy* and *phroneō*.
> **Chloe:** *(word studies)* Here you go. Cross-references too?
> **You:** Please. And three illustrations for chapter 4.

Say things like: "go deeper," "just the essentials," "give me the main views,"
"add cross-references," "where is that on a map?", "who said that quote?"

---

## Things you can ask Chloe for

- "Prepare everything on ___." (all eleven pieces at once)
- "Historical background / an outline for ___."
- "A word study on ___." (Greek or Hebrew)
- "Cross-references for ___."
- "Maps and geography for ___." / "Where is ___?"
- "Any archaeology that lights up ___?"
- "Illustrations / quotes / application ideas for ___."
- "What are the difficult verses in ___, and the main views?"
- "Discussion questions for a small group on ___."

---

## What Chloe promises

- **She never makes things up.** Not a verse, a Greek word, a date, an
  archaeological find, or a quote. If she can't confirm something, she says so and
  offers to verify — a flagged gap instead of a confident guess.
- **She cites and marks confidence.** Quotes get an author; archaeology gets a
  site; she tells you what's well-established versus one scholarly view.
- **She stays in your lane.** She quotes your translation (NKJV by default) and
  stays inside your doctrinal frame — both set in `chloe/research-preferences.md`.

---

## What Chloe remembers

- **Your preferences** — saved in `chloe/research-preferences.md`: default
  translation, doctrinal frame, how deep to go, and your trusted sources. Chloe
  reads it every time. The more you set, the better every brief fits you.
- **Her output** — anything she saves goes in `chloe/output/` so you can find it
  later.

Within one conversation Chloe remembers everything you've said. Across separate
conversations, the **preferences file is her long-term memory** — that's why it's
a file you can keep improving.

---

## Making Chloe better over time

Tell her your go-to commentaries, the depth you like, and which pieces to always
include or skip. She'll fold it into your preferences so every future brief fits
how you study.
